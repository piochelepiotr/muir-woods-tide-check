WHY THE TIDE TABLE WAS 1.51 FT LOW

The ocean had a head start.

On October 29 at 1:42 PM PDT, NOAA’s San Francisco gauge recorded 8.046 ft MLLW against an astronomical tide prediction of 6.534 ft. The station, timestamp, units and datum match. The extra 1.512 ft was real water beyond the tide-table prediction.

NOAA attributes the late-October regional event to El Niño–driven Kelvin waves and unusually warm water expanding and raising the sea level. Our matched gauge data shows elevated levels across several days and at Point Reyes and Alameda too. This is evidence of a regional sea-level anomaly; it does not identify exactly how much each mechanism contributed.

We now use NOAA’s separate P-ETSS total-water forecast and its 10th–90th percentile ensemble band. It models surge and adds a recent observational bias adjustment. The planning check uses the upper bound when available. A fixed +0.5 ft allowance alone would have been too small for this event. Even an ensemble band can miss outcomes and does not model this road’s drainage.

The evidence we still need ＋

To measure missed closures, false alarms and drainage delays, we need location-specific closure and reopening times, plus verified open periods across both ordinary and high tides. The official 511 API offers up to 1,000 archived traffic events with an API token; no token is configured. More history can be requested from 511. No API archive has been imported here.

Going forward, this site preserves timestamped public Caltrans/CHP reports and alert snapshots on the repository’s data-history branch. A missing or removed report is recorded as unverified; it never becomes a confirmed-open observation. Sampling about every 30 minutes can miss short closures.

The road’s level

The county’s January 2021 engineering study reports SR‑1 elevations from just under 6 to 8 ft NAVD88 near the lot. Its summary of 2013 observations places shoulder encroachment near 5.6 ft MLLW and lane encroachment near 6.5 ft MLLW. These historical markers are not a current road survey.

02

The water’s level

NOAA station 9414290 at San Francisco is the study’s reference, about 7 miles south. We use its 6-minute predictions, high/low events and preliminary observations, all in feet MLLW. The gauge does not measure water on the connector.

03

What takes time

Valves, culverts and ponding affect drainage. No validated fixed drain-down duration was found. The adjustable 2-hour buffer is an assumption. Caltrans reopens when tides subside and crews judge the roadway safe.

Sources, freshness & decision rules ＋

“Lower tide exposure” requires a fresh NOAA P-ETSS forecast throughout the entire ±30-minute crossing window and preceding clearance buffer, with its upper (90th-percentile) bound and the tide scenario both below the shoulder marker; fresh weather and road feeds; hourly forecast coverage; no matching flood alert, rain forecast, or nearby closure/flood wording; and a usable observed comparison for a crossing in the next 6 hours. It is never a road-clearance signal. Road reports are incomplete and can omit ramp closures. P-ETSS includes surge, tide and an observational bias adjustment. The 10th–90th percentile spread is model guidance, not a guaranteed 80% coverage interval and not a road-closure probability. Its hourly bounds are linearly interpolated; no extrapolation is used. Outside forecast coverage, tide scenarios remain available but road conditions stay unverified. Weather beyond the hourly forecast range also remains unverified.

NOAA observations with a flagged quality field are excluded from the recent excess calculation; preliminary values can change. Alert validity dates are matched to your crossing. Feed retrieval older than 90 minutes is marked stale. Predictions remain valid for their published dates. Road/incident reports refresh through GitHub Actions about every 30 minutes; schedules can be delayed. NOAA and weather feeds also refresh in your browser every 5 minutes while this tab is open.
